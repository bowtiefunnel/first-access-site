Affiliate Disclosure

Last updated: November 15, 2026

In short: businesses that use First Access may earn a commission when a client buys a product they recommend. It never costs the client more.

How recommendations work

Client cards can include product picks from the business that performed your service, matched to that service and to your stage of recovery. Each pick is approved by the business before you see it.

Commissions

Some product links are affiliate links. If you buy through one, the business that recommended it may earn a commission from the brand or retailer's affiliate program. The price you pay is the same either way. First Access does not take a share of these commissions.

Some businesses also sell products they stock themselves. Those links go to the business's own store.

Where you'll see it

Every product pick on a card carries a short disclosure, as the U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) requires, so you always know when a link may earn a commission.

Where you buy

Purchases happen on the seller's own website, not inside First Access. The seller handles payment, shipping, returns and customer service under its own policies.

Program-specific statements

Some affiliate programs require their own wording. For example, when a business uses the Amazon Associates program, its cards state: “As an Amazon Associate, [business name] earns from qualifying purchases.”

Not medical advice

Recommendations come from the professional who treated you and are general product suggestions. They are not medical advice. If you have a reaction or concern, contact the business or a medical professional.
